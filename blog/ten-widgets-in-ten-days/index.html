Ten widgets in ten days

12 January 2024

On 12 January 2024 I started a small experiment: design one widget concept for Nothing OS every day for ten days. Not finished apps — just the idea of what each widget could be, drawn and animated so people could see it.

The brief was wide on purpose. Some days were useful, some were silly, and one was a game. That mix is what made the series worth doing.

The ten

  • Recorder — a one-tap voice recorder that sits quietly on the home screen.
  • Moon phases — the current phase of the moon, as a dark, calm widget.
  • Battery dots — charge left, shown as a grid of dots instead of a bar.
  • Calendar — the month at a glance, with just enough detail.
  • FM Radio — a tuning dial and preset stations.
  • Audio switcher — flip between speakers, buds, and other outputs.
  • Breath — a slow breathing guide for a minute of quiet.
  • GIF — a looping GIF frame on the home screen.
  • Now Playing — the current track, with controls that fit the look.
  • Snake game — the classic, shrunk down to a widget.

Concepts, not products

I was clear from the start that these were explorations. Making a concept real is a different job: permissions, performance, battery, edge cases. A ten-second animation can hide a month of work.

But the series did something I did not plan for. It showed me which ideas people actually wanted. The Snake widget was the loudest signal by far.

What happened next

Months later, the Snake widget shipped for NothingOS. Something that started as day ten of a sketch challenge became a thing people use on their phones. It is still the project I get asked about most.

I also released the Figma and After Effects files for the series, so other people could learn from them or build their own versions.

I ran the experiment again a year later, with a tighter brief. You can read about that in Ten widgets, ten days: round two.
