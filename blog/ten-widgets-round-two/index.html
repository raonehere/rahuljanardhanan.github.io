Ten widgets, ten days: round two

4 February 2025

A year after the first series, I ran it again. This time the brief was tighter: every concept had to be something you would actually keep on your home screen. Less fantasy, more daily use.

Same rules, though. One idea a day for ten days, designed and animated so people could see how it would feel.

The ten

  • Caffeinate — keep the screen awake for a set stretch of time.
  • Clipboard — copied text history, grouped by day and easy to search.
  • Pomodoro — a focus timer that fits the Nothing look.
  • Walkie Talkie — push-to-talk voice memos, right from the home screen.
  • Sun Dial — time read as light and shadow.
  • Age in Motion — your age, always moving.
  • Chrono — a stopwatch with a little personality.
  • Sys Info — CPU, RAM, storage, network, and battery at a glance.
  • Spinner — a playful take on the Nothing spinner.
  • World Clock — several cities at once.

From sketches to software

The second series was useful in a different way. It stopped being only an exploration and started feeding real work. Caffeinate, Clipboard, Walkie Talkie, System Info, and the music player all exist now in Glyph Lab.

Not every idea survives that trip. Some look great as a loop and fall apart when you have to handle permissions, battery, and a hundred phone models. The ones that make it are the ones where the concept was simple enough to build well.

The lesson from both series is the same: a small, public constraint is a good way to find out what is worth making.
