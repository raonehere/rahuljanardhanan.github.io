The Glyph Matrix is the grid of LEDs on the back of newer Nothing phones. It is a tiny screen — a few dozen dots — and that is exactly what makes it fun. You cannot show everything, so every idea has to survive being reduced to dots.

For ten days I designed one toy a day for it. Same format as the widget series: one concept, animated so people could see how it would move.

## The ten

- **Charging Hamster** — a little hamster that runs when you plug in, and shows the battery while it goes.
- **Guitar Tuner** — a tuner that turns the matrix into a simple readout.
- **Glyph Hunt** — a small hunt played on the LEDs.
- **Quiet Bud** — a plant buddy that reacts to your screen time.
- **Bat Signal** — a light in the dark, for no practical reason at all.
- **Zen Mode** — a slow, quiet pattern for when you want the phone to disappear.
- **Ball Maze** — a tiny maze you tilt your way through.
- **Cave Explorer** — a dot-sized adventure in the dark.
- **Walkie Talkie** — the push-to-talk idea, shrunk to the matrix.
- **Snake Matrix** — the classic game, back on the LEDs.

## Designing for dots

The constraint is the whole design. There is no text, no colour, and very little space. A face can be six dots. A running hamster can be a wheel and two legs. If an idea needs more than that, it is not a Glyph toy yet.

It also changes how you think about motion. At this size, timing matters more than detail. A single dot arriving a fraction late can be the whole joke.

## What shipped

Some of these made it into [Glyph Lab](../glyph-lab/) as real, playable toys, including the Charging Hamster and Glyph Snake. Others stayed as concepts, which is fine. The series is a way to find the ones worth building.
